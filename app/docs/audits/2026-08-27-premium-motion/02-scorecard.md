# Rams scorecard

Scale: 0 absent, 1 weak, 2 credible, 3 exemplary.

1. Good design is innovative: 1/3  
   Evidence: the static editorial split is competent but familiar. The Red Thread does not yet behave as intelligence lineage.

2. Good design makes a product useful: 3/3  
   Evidence: the screen moves directly from signal to why now, proof, precedent and response.

3. Good design is aesthetic: 1/3  
   Evidence: the system is consistent, but the actual result is flat, evenly weighted and under-art-directed.

4. Good design makes a product understandable: 3/3  
   Evidence: one lead, explicit receipts, evidence state and decision sequence are immediately identifiable.

5. Good design is unobtrusive: 3/3  
   Evidence: no decorative dashboard chrome competes with the evidence.

6. Good design is honest: 3/3  
   Evidence: unavailable, thin, contradictory, unchecked, stale, error and no-discovery states retain their actual meaning.

7. Good design is long-lasting: 2/3  
   Evidence: black, paper and red are durable, but generic editorial web typography weakens identity.

8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail: 2/3  
   Evidence: state, focus and mobile work are covered, but title hover, scroll rhythm and chapter transitions are unfinished.

9. Good design is environmentally friendly: 3/3  
   Evidence: no idle animation, no external font request, modest bundle, reduced-motion support.

10. Good design is as little design as possible: 2/3  
    Evidence: the primary screen is restrained, but repeated red rules and equal chapter treatment can be reduced.

Total: 23/30.
