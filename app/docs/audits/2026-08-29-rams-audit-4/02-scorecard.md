Scale: 0 absent, 1 weak, 2 credible, 3 exemplary.

Audited surface: staging revision `listening-post-staging-00105-dbd`, branch `feat/42-redesign-staging`, commit `d010c75`. Read: the deployed entry screen, the fixture-rendered Briefing at 1440 and 390, the Discover, Compare, Source Lab and cited-answer sources, and the executed suites.

1. **Good design is innovative: 2/3**
   The Live Margin is a real interface idea rather than a dashboard restyle: the lead holds while four decision chapters advance and a red thread marks position. It is not exemplary because the thread still marks reading position more than evidence relationship; the constitution asks it to mark where two sources agree or contradict, and only readiness carries that today.

2. **Good design makes a product useful: 3/3**
   Briefing moves signal, why now, receipts, precedent, possible response in one path. Discover filters the admitted index by readiness and mode with honest counts. Compare reads the same released run through aligned strips. The strategist never picks an engine topic or score to get there.

3. **Good design is aesthetic: 2/3**
   The editorial system holds: one red, ink and paper, serif for interpretation, mono for provenance, radius 0, two elevations, eight type sizes. It is not exemplary while three legacy stylesheets still carry gradients and rounded cards for routes awaiting migration, and while the Ogilvy brand faces remain unavailable so the fallback Baskerville stack carries display type.

4. **Good design makes a product understandable: 3/3**
   Engine measurements read as words a strategist uses. Evidence readiness is a structural state, not a badge. Unmeasured is written as unmeasured. No score, dataset or table name reaches a reader.

5. **Good design is unobtrusive: 3/3**
   The boot splash, brand dots, ticker, blinking markers, hover lifts and the motion selector are gone. One motion idea remains: a red rule wipes under what you are about to open, 220ms, keyboard focus treated like a pointer. Nothing animates while the reader is idle on a redesigned surface.

6. **Good design is honest: 3/3**
   No demographic claim ships; the audience refusal states why. A recommendation cannot render ready without receipts. Refusal payloads render as refusals. The credit budget reports unmeasured spend rather than zero. The golden-task harness rejects causal, predictive and polling language in approved copy.

7. **Good design is long-lasting: 2/3**
   The identity is editorial rather than fashionable, and the palette carries meaning rather than trend. Held at 2 because the product still runs on a hash router with local storage state, and because six of the surfaces it promises are refusal stubs whose real shape is unproven.

8. **Good design is thorough down to the last detail: 3/3**
   The state matrix is executed rather than asserted, and it was mutation-tested: one mutation was caught, a second was not, the assertion was rewritten, and the same mutation now fails it. The overclaim detector carries its own self-tests. At 390px the recommendation begins 378px down and the document does not overflow (scrollWidth 375 against innerWidth 390).

9. **Good design is environmentally friendly: 3/3**
   No external font request. Lighthouse at this revision: desktop performance 91, mobile 90, both with CLS 0 and total blocking time 0 ms, LCP 0.8 s desktop and 2.9 s mobile. Accessibility 96 desktop, 100 mobile, best practices 100 on both.

10. **Good design is as little design as possible: 2/3**
    Three grammars carry the redesigned surfaces, three dead destinations are deleted, the duplicate Compare ranking is gone and the raw-font-size ratchet fell from 787 to 704. Held at 2 because the route wall is shorter, not short: Browse, Method, Network, Lexicon, My board, Journey map, seeds, seed path, listen, topic and research still exist as destinations.

**Total: 26/30.** No principle scores zero.
